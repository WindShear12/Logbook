Which capacity applies?
Pilot in command (PIC)
You were the pilot in command. Only one pilot can log PIC for a flight. A flight instructor giving instruction may log PIC.
PICUS
You hold a pilot licence, performed all PIC duties, and either an operator with training and checking responsibilities authorised the PIC to supervise you, or you flew under the supervision of an instructor or examiner without receiving training. This covers assessment (not training) on a flight review, endorsement check flight or flight test.
Co-pilot
Time in an aircraft that must be operated by at least 2 pilots.
Dual
You received flight training from a flight instructor, including any part of a flight review where you were trained rather than assessed.
Second pilot in a single-pilot aircraft
Each pilot logs only the role they carried out. A second pilot who is not PIC, PICUS or receiving training cannot log the time. A safety pilot keeping a lookout logs PIC only if they were the designated PIC, agreed before the flight.

    What CASA expects of your logbook

    • Keep your logbook for 7 years from the last date of entry, with each entry unaltered. Entries here are never overwritten: amendments keep the original, and voided entries stay visible.
    • If CASA asks you to correct an entry, you must do so within 14 days of receiving the notice. Use Amend on the entry and quote the notice as the reason.
    • If CASA directs you to produce your logbook, you must provide it within 7 days. For an electronic logbook you must produce a printed copy and certify each page as a true copy.
    • A lost logbook can be reconstructed from other records, such as your employer's or flying school's, with a statutory declaration for the records entered.

    Source: CASA, Keeping records in flight crew logbooks. See also the Part 61 Flight crew licensing plain English guide.
